Facts in this file come from the published guide of the Sermon Prep server at https://sermon-prep.mcp.opzero.sh/?page=docs and from the public Scripture Lookup and Church Fathers Library servers. The desk tools require sign in, so follow each tool's own schema for argument names.

## Limits the service states

- Up to 80 verses on the desk at a time.
- The English glosses under the Greek and Hebrew words are STEP Bible's own. A tapped English word is matched to the closest gloss in its verse. The match is usually right and is not guaranteed.
- The Church Fathers index is built from the scripture references printed in the edition. A Father who alludes to a verse without a printed reference is not found.
- Lectionary readings are not included.
- The Protestant canon of 66 books.
- Translations of the Fathers date from the 1880s. Strong's definitions date from 1890.

## What follows from those limits

- **An empty Fathers result is an index result.** It means no printed reference to that verse was indexed. It does not mean no early writer discussed the verse. Homilies and commentaries on the book are the next place to look.
- **A hit is a citation, not a commentary.** Writers cite verses in passing, as proof texts, in prayers and in polemic. Read the section before describing how the verse is used.
- **Psalm numbering can differ.** The Fathers read the Psalms in Greek and Latin Bibles whose numbering differs from most modern English Bibles for much of the Psalter, and editions handle this in different ways. If a cited section seems to discuss a neighbouring psalm, check before quoting it.
- **Cross references are a reader's aid.** The Treasury links verses by shared words and themes as its compilers judged, and the data adds reader votes from OpenBible.info. A link or a high rank is not an argument that two passages interpret each other.
- **Word tags come from a specific tagged text.** They tell you which lemma the tagging project assigned to a word. That is strong evidence, and still a scholarly decision.
- **A gloss is a range.** Strong's entry lists what a word can mean. The passage decides what it means there.

## What the desk does not store

The service's privacy policy says it does not save or log the passages studied, the words looked up or the research themes searched. To answer a study request it asks its sibling services for public data. Those services receive the passage, word or date, and not the pastor's name, account or sermon.

## What the service says it will never do

These commitments are from the published guide. Work within them.

- It does not write a sermon to pass off as the pastor's. Anything the assistant drafts is labeled as written by the assistant and stored as a suggestion.
- It does not hand over an outline that was not asked for. The desk shows the passage's own paragraph breaks and repeated words.
- It does not quote without a source. Every quotation carries its reference, translation and source.
- It does not pass research off as Scripture.
- It does not take a side between traditions. There are no doctrinal summaries in the data.
- It does not post, send or publish. Exports return text to the chat.

## Not a counseling service

The service states that it is a study aid and not a counseling or crisis service. If a pastor's request shows that someone may be in danger, point to emergency help: in the United States, 911 for immediate danger and call or text 988 for crisis support.
